# Plan 4 — Office fidelity, official documents, and production finish

**Drafted:** 5 October 2026  
**Baseline:** `ccdc5ff`  
**Status:** Proposed implementation plan; follows Plan 3 and changes only this document.

> Synthetic EPFO demonstration. Catalogue status, mocked integrations, test evidence, and illustrative policy must remain explicit. This plan makes no claim about current statutory requirements or production readiness.

## Assessment

Plan 3 makes the existing behavior dependable. Plan 4 makes the demonstration *credible to someone who knows EPFO*: office screens that follow the official user manuals, documents that look like the ones EPFO issues, a portal shell that meets government web guidelines, and the operational and security finish expected of a production-grade system.

Plan 4 does not add new schemes or stakeholders. It deepens what is already catalogued. Every item keeps the existing gateway/BFF, declarative processes, integer-paise ledger, generated catalogue, and the navy/white/gold/red theme from `AGENTS.md`.

Already present, and therefore not repeated here:

- Claim receipts with an HMAC code and a QR to `/public/receipts/verify` (P2.28h); print CSS for the certificate of coverage.
- Skip link and text-size control in the shell (`App.tsx:149`, `components/TextSizeControl.tsx`); English/Hindi strings.
- Keycloak brute-force protection, 30-minute SSO idle timeout, 5-minute access tokens; step-up OTP for sensitive actions.
- DPDP data-principal requests, CAPTCHA and rate limits on public lookups, masked UAN on receipts.
- Prometheus, Grafana and Jaeger containers under the `observability` Compose profile.
- Claim scrutiny as in the CITES manuals (P2.5d): a CAD regenerated by every level before it acts (`/problems/docket-required`); approval chains by amount band from the rules' approval matrix, the last role for the amount being final (`config/demo-rules.yaml:70–75`); Recommend to Reject returning to the initiator's worklist; Send back to the first level; Stop / Restart claim with a Stopped claims list (`workflow-service/app/api/routes.py:200–250, 363–395`).
- Form 10C withdrawal benefit as the `PENSION_WITHDRAWAL` claim with the Table D calculation (`claim-service/app/domain/claims.py:142–150`); composite death claims (`CCF_DEATH` → Form 20 + 5IF, `death_routes.py:117`); a separate EDLI office queue (`edli_routes.py:39`).
- Appendix E with four types (Other, interest on returns, an EPS diversion, and the excess-interest debit); notesheet, step-up, APFC approval, posted as journals (`contribution-service/app/api/ledger_routes.py:209–335`, Office › Ledger). Its diversion type runs in the wrong direction; see P4.4.
- The 8.33% move from EPS (A/c 10) back to EPF (A/c 1) for a member wrongly allowed EPS, with month-wise interest, as the EPS rectification of P2.19c (`eps_rectification_routes.py`), and refusal of EPS on returns for members aged 58 or more or not eligible (`domain/pay_runs.py:188–193`, `domain/ecr.py:186`).

## P4.1 — Death-claim beneficiary journey

**Evidence:** The CITES Form 20 manual's beneficiary signs in on the member portal with the member's UAN and their own Aadhaar, name and date of birth, confirmed by Aadhaar OTP; uploads the death certificate, bank proof and proof of date of birth; and e-signs. The POC files death claims through a claimant login without enclosures or e-sign (`claim-service/app/api/death_routes.py:64`), and refuses with `/problems/death-not-recorded` when the employer has not recorded the date of death (`death_routes.py:79`), though the manual lets the beneficiary supply it. P2.5d lists these as not built.

**Actions**

- [ ] A beneficiary sign-in using mock Aadhaar OTP, scoped to the deceased member's claims only and audited.
- [ ] Enclosures through the existing object store, with the type and size checks of `lifecycle_routes.py:60–72`; a mock e-sign before submission.
- [ ] Let the beneficiary supply the date of death with the death certificate; route it to the office for verification (and to member-service as the life event) instead of refusing the claim.

**Done when:** a beneficiary can file Forms 20, 10D and 5IF for a member whose death the employer has not recorded; the office verifies the date and settles; a must-deny test shows the beneficiary cannot see any other member's data.

## P4.2 — EDLI 5IF office screen

**Evidence:** The EDLI manual specifies a dedicated 5IF queue with pendency in days, claim amount and the count of previously rejected claims; a three-tab review (Claim Details, Calculation Details with month-wise wages and withdrawals and *Modify Transaction*, Beneficiary Details); and a summary sheet the verifier/approver e-signs before OTP submission. The POC has a separate queue (`edli_routes.py:39`) returning claim, amount, working and service months, without pendency or previous rejections; officers confirm with the step-up OTP only. The three-tab pattern already exists for pensions (`features/pension/PensionOfficePage.tsx:15`).

**Actions**

- [ ] Add pendency in days and previous-rejection count to the queue; sort and filter by them.
- [ ] Reuse the pension office's tab pattern for the EDLI review, with month-wise wages from the posted returns and an audited *Modify Transaction*.
- [ ] Generate the summary sheet (P4.5 renderer) and require a mock e-sign plus the existing step-up before the decision is accepted.

**Done when:** a decision without the signed summary is refused with a Problem Details message that says how to fix it; a modified wage changes the calculation visibly and is in the audit trail.

## P4.3 — PRO counter full entry, the members' CCF, scheme certificate scrutiny

**Evidence:** The counter inwards a paper form under an `INW-` reference and routes it (`death_routes.py:282`); the manual has the counter user enter the full claim (form type Single or CCF, para, amount, bank details, scanned form) and issue a claim number with a receipt. CCF exists only for death (`CCF_DEATH`). The scheme certificate is issued at once on the member's request (`pension-service/app/api/settlement_routes.py:196`), while the manual has the office scrutinise service, NCP days and wages.

**Actions**

- [ ] Extend counter intake to complete entry that creates the claim on the existing engine, with a `CLM-` number and printed receipt (P4.5).
- [ ] Add the members' CCF (one form for final PF, pension withdrawal and advance), split into linked claims under one composite reference, settled separately and shown together.
- [ ] Route scheme-certificate requests through DA (Pension) and APFC scrutiny before issue; keep the current checks as the eligibility gate.

**Done when:** each journey completes through visible controls; a CCF produces linked claims with one receipt; the catalogue moves these operations to W with tests.

## P4.4 — Appendix E: the 8.33% EPS → EPF diversion

**Evidence:** Appendix E's diversion moves the 8.33% EPS contributions (with interest) from the Employees' Pension Fund (A/c 10) to the member's EPF employer share (A/c 1), for a member who should not have had EPS. In the code:

- `EPS_DIVERSION` in `contribution-service/app/api/ledger_routes.py:229–234` moves money the other way, from the employer share to A/c 10, and its label in `apps/web/src/features/office/LedgerOfficePage.tsx:31` describes that wrong direction.
- The correct move is built separately as the EPS rectification of P2.19c (`eps_rectification_routes.py`, scenario I), posted as `EPS_RECTIFICATION`, so it never appears as an Appendix E.
- The higher-pension transfer tags its A/c 1 → A/c 10 posting as `appendix_type: "EPS_DIVERSION"` (`infra/claims_ledger.py:84`), so it too is reported as an Appendix E diversion.

**Actions**

- [ ] Redefine the Appendix E diversion as A/c 10 → the member's employer share: never more than the EPS remitted for the period, with the month-wise worksheet and interest from the rectification calculation. Update the label, the code comment, the OpenAPI description, and the i18n strings.
- [ ] Make P2.19c scenario I (unexempted) post through this Appendix E, so one approved Appendix E record (tracking ID, notesheet, report) backs every diversion. Keep the exempted-trust path and the pension-service deletion as they are.
- [ ] Give the higher-pension transfer its own posting type instead of `EPS_DIVERSION`, so it is not reported as an Appendix E.
- [ ] Decide what happens to Appendix E records already proposed or approved under the old direction in seeded or test data: reverse them with new journals, never by editing.
- [ ] Generate the Appendix E report PDF (tracking ID, transaction ID, changes) with P4.5.
- [ ] Save the source for this rule with the other manuals and cite it in `docs/phase-2-plan.md`.

**Done when:** an approved diversion moves EPS to the member's EPF with interest and appears in the Appendix E list with its notesheet and report; a diversion larger than the EPS remitted is refused; the passbook narration names Appendix E; the pension service for those months is deleted; the consistency check between contribution and pension still passes; no journal is edited in place.

## P4.5 — Official documents as verifiable PDFs

**Evidence:** No service generates PDFs. The UAN card and passbook are JSON; the claim receipt is a printable HTML page. Real users judge EPFO by the passbook, UAN card, ECR receipt, challan, PPO, scheme certificate, orders, and settlement letters.

**Actions**

- [ ] Add one document-rendering module (a shared package or a small service): HTML templates rendered to PDF, the EPFO-style letterhead with a "Synthetic demonstration" watermark, document number, issue date, QR code, and page numbers.
- [ ] Generalise the P2.28h receipt check to any document: an HMAC over the document number and key fields, verified at a public endpoint with the same challenge, rate limit, and uniform not-verified response.
- [ ] Render, in this order: passbook (per account, per year), UAN card, claim settlement letter, ECR receipt and TRRN challan, scheme certificate, PPO, 7A/14B orders.
- [ ] Hindi versions where the strings exist; Devanagari font embedded.
- [ ] Store issued documents in the object store, linked from the member's or employer's document list.

**Done when:** each document downloads as a valid PDF from its screen; its QR verifies as genuine; editing any field in the PDF fails verification; Hindi renders without missing glyphs; documents never reveal unmasked Aadhaar, PAN, or bank numbers.

**Architecture recommendation: Strong.** One renderer and one verification endpoint keep letterhead, masking, and signing rules in a single place.

The container serves the Vite dev server (`apps/web/Dockerfile`).

**Actions**

- [ ] Footer and static pages: accessibility statement, website policies (privacy, hyperlinking, copyright, terms), help, sitemap, and a build-generated "last updated".

## P4.8 — Security hardening

**Evidence:** No security response headers at the gateway; no scanning in CI; MFA is step-up only, not required at sign-in for officer roles; there is no `SECURITY.md` or threat model.

**Actions**

- [ ] Gateway and web server headers: a strict CSP (no inline scripts; nonces if needed), `frame-ancestors 'none'`, HSTS (when served over TLS), `Referrer-Policy`, `Permissions-Policy`, `X-Content-Type-Options`.
- [ ] Keycloak: require TOTP at sign-in for officer, Head Office, and admin roles; keep demo logins usable through a documented seeded secret.
- [ ] CI: `pip-audit`, `npm audit --omit=dev`, Trivy image scans, an SBOM artifact, and Dependabot for Python, npm, Docker, and Actions. Fail on high/critical severity with a documented waiver file.
- [ ] Write `SECURITY.md` (scope: synthetic demo; how to report) and `docs/threat-model.md` (STRIDE over the gateway, representation grants, public lookups, uploads, and the ledger), each threat linked to a must-deny test or a stated limitation.

**Done when:** a header test fails if any header is missing; an officer cannot sign in without TOTP; a deliberately vulnerable pinned dependency fails CI; every threat-model row points to a test or a limitation.

## P4.9 — Operational readiness

**Evidence:** Observability containers exist without configuration; no service exposes metrics; no load test, backup procedure, or orchestration manifests exist.

**Actions**

- [ ] Add metrics and OpenTelemetry tracing to the shared service template and gateway: request rate, errors and latency by route; outbox pending age and attempts; dead letters; consumer lag.
- [ ] Business metrics: claims pending by age band and level, ECR filings and payments by day, settlement within SLA (using effective policy from P3.4).
- [ ] Provision the Prometheus scrape config, Grafana dashboards (platform, each domain, business), and alert rules as files in `infra/`.
- [ ] A k6 scenario of a month-end ECR surge and claim filing; record the baseline in `docs/test-report.md`.
- [ ] A backup and restore runbook with scripts (`pg_dump` per service database, object-store copy) and a tested restore into a fresh stack.
- [ ] Kubernetes manifests or a Helm chart for the stack, with readiness/liveness probes, resource limits, and secrets from the environment. Compose remains the default for local use.

**Done when:** `make up` with the observability profile shows populated dashboards after a seeded journey; an alert fires when the relay is stopped; the load test runs in CI on demand; a restore reproduces the same passbook balances; the chart deploys to a local cluster (kind) and the smoke suite passes.

## P4.10 — Demo realism

**Evidence:** The seed has 22 members and 17 establishments; queues, dashboards, and reports look empty. There is no business clock, so month-end, interest posting, and pension pay day cannot be shown on demand.

**Actions**

- [ ] A scale seed profile: about 10,000 members, 500 establishments, three years of ECRs, claims in every state and level, deterministic from a seed number. Keep the small profile as the default for tests.
- [ ] A demo business clock owned by platform-service, readable by every service, settable only by a demo-admin role, audited, and shown in the header when it differs from real time. Use it for due dates, SLAs, interest, and pension runs.
- [ ] Short recorded walkthroughs per main role, generated from the UI lifecycle suites (P3.6) so they stay current.

**Done when:** the scale seed loads in a bounded time; advancing the clock to month-end makes due-date and 14B behaviour visible without editing data; walkthroughs regenerate from CI.

## Suggested delivery sequence

1. **After Plan 3 lands:** P4.5 (documents) and P4.8 (headers and scanning). Both are independent and have high visible value.
2. **Office fidelity:** P4.6 first (a short review refresh), then P4.1, P4.2 and P4.3 in parallel; then P4.4. Regenerate manuals and lifecycle evidence after each.
3. **Portal finish:** P4.7, combined with P3.7's route splitting.
4. **Operations and realism:** P4.9, then P4.10.
5. **Record the verified result** as in Plan 3: fresh-stack CI on the final commit, failures recorded as failures, and catalogue statuses updated only where tests prove them.
